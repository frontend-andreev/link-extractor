How to extract all links from a large page with 10,000+ links

Add to Chrome — it's free

Short answer: open the page, let it load all its content, click the Link Extractor icon and use Copy URLs or CSV. Copying and the file include every link in the list, with nothing cut off. In our tests a page with 10,000 links is listed in about a second, and the list still scrolls and filters without freezing.

Who this is for

People who work with long pages: sitemaps and archive pages, directories and catalogs, link lists, documentation indexes, long forum threads, feeds that keep loading as you scroll. Many link extractors slow down, freeze the tab or quietly stop at a few hundred or a few thousand links there.

What matters on a big page

  • A complete result. If the tool stops early, it should say so, not hand you part of the page as if it were all of it.
  • The page loaded first. Links that appear only after scrolling or “Show more” are not in the page yet when you start.
  • A responsive list, so you can search and filter tens of thousands of rows.
  • Duplicates merged: on big pages the same links repeat a lot.

Step by step

  1. Open the page. If it loads more as you scroll, scroll to the end, or click “Show more” until everything you need is on the page.
  2. Click the Link Extractor icon, or press Alt+Shift+L (on a Mac, Ctrl+Shift+L). The side panel lists the links in page order; repeats are one row with a counter.
  3. If you scrolled further after opening the panel, press Refresh in the panel to add what has loaded since.
  4. Optional: type in the search box or open Filters & settings to keep only the links you need, for example one domain or only external links.
  5. Click Copy URLs to copy them, one per line, or CSV to download a file.

Limits, said plainly

  • The panel reads up to 200,000 links from a page, and as many from each embedded frame. If a page has more, the panel says Only the first 200,000 links are in the list.
  • Links in closed menus and hidden blocks are left out by default, and the panel shows how many there are. +N hidden adds them.
  • Excel holds at most 32,767 characters in a cell. If a URL or link text is longer, that cell is cut in the CSV and the panel tells you how many cells were cut.
  • Links inside frames embedded from other sites need your permission; the panel says which part of the page it could not read.

When another tool is the better choice

  • Links from many pages of a site. Link Extractor does not crawl: it reads the one tab you have open. For a whole site, use a site audit tool such as Screaming Frog SEO Spider.

Questions

Will the tab freeze while it reads 10,000+ links?

No. The panel draws only the rows you can see, so scrolling and filtering stay fast even with tens of thousands of links.

Is the copy cut off at some number of rows?

No. Copy URLs and CSV take every link in the list, after your search and filters.

Does it get links that load with infinite scroll?

Yes, once they are on the page. Scroll until they have loaded, then press Refresh. It does not scroll the page for you.
