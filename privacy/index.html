Privacy Policy — Link Extractor

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Link Extractor is a Chrome extension that lists the links of the page you are viewing, so you can filter, copy or save them as a CSV file. It works entirely inside your browser.

The short version

  • The extension itself collects no personal data and makes no network requests.
  • The links you extract never leave your browser. They go only where you send them yourself: your clipboard or a file you download.
  • The extension has no analytics, no tracking and no ads.
  • Our website, link-extractor.com, counts the install, a 1–3 star rating and the removal of the extension, with the answers you choose to give there. Each event carries a random ID kept in your browser by the website. With the IP address PostHog receives, this ID may count as pseudonymous personal data under laws such as the GDPR. We do not ask for your name or email, do not try to connect this ID to them and never sell it; it goes only to PostHog, which processes it for us. It stays after you remove the extension; clearing the site data of link-extractor.com deletes it. See "Our website" below.

What the extension reads

The extension reads a page only when you ask it to: when you click its icon, press its keyboard shortcut, or choose "Extract links from the selection" in the right-click menu. It reads the links of that page (their addresses, text and a few attributes such as rel) and shows them in Chrome's side panel. While the panel is open and automatic updates are on, it reads the tab again when the tab goes to another page, so the list matches what you see. It does not change the page; the only exception is a short highlight of a link when you ask the extension to show that link on the page.

The list exists only while the side panel is open. It is not saved anywhere and is gone when you close the panel.

Permissions and why they are needed

Requested at install:

  • activeTab — read the tab you are on, and only after you click the icon or use the shortcut or the menu item.
  • scripting — run the small script that reads the links of that tab.
  • sidePanel — show the list in Chrome's side panel.
  • contextMenus — add "Extract links from the selection" to the right-click menu.
  • storage — remember your settings (see below).

Optional, asked only when you turn on a feature that needs it. You can say no, and you can take it back at any time in Chrome's extension settings:

  • Access to all sites — two features use it. Automatic list updates after the tab moves to another site: Chrome does not let an extension ask for "just this new site", so the only choice is all sites. And links inside embedded blocks (frames) loaded from other sites: here the extension asks for those sites only. Chrome describes this access as "Read and change all your data on all websites"; the extension still only reads links, and only from the tab you are using it on.

What is stored on your device

In Chrome's extension storage, on your computer only:

  • your settings: how duplicates are handled, address options (unwrapping redirect links, removing tracking parameters, readable addresses), the CSV separator, the copy format, whether the list updates automatically, and whether you declined access to all sites;
  • whether the extension has already asked you to rate it and whether you have rated it. The number of stars is not stored;
  • for a moment, while the side panel opens: which tab to read and whether to read the whole page or the selection. Chrome deletes this when the browser closes.

Your filters and the links themselves are never stored. Removing the extension deletes everything listed here. It does not delete the random ID that our website keeps in its own storage in your browser (see "The random ID" below); to delete that too, clear the site data of link-extractor.com in Chrome's settings.

Network requests

None. The extension does not contact any server, ours or anyone else's. Unwrapping redirect links and removing tracking parameters are done by reading the address itself, without opening the link.

Our website

Three pages of our website, link-extractor.com, open from the extension. They are pages on the internet, so your browser loads them like any other website.

  • Welcome page, once, right after you install the extension: https://link-extractor.com/welcome/. The address includes the extension's ID, your Chrome interface language and whether the extension icon is pinned to the toolbar, so the page can show the right instructions. The page can ask the extension one thing: whether its icon is pinned. It does not open on updates, and does not open when your organization installs the extension for you.
  • "What went wrong?" page, when you rate the extension 1 to 3 stars in its panel: https://link-extractor.com/feedback/. The address includes the stars you gave, where you gave them (the question in the panel or the line under its buttons), the extension's version, your Chrome interface language and two fixed labels that show the extension opened the page (utm_source=extension, utm_medium=rating). 4 or 5 stars open the extension's reviews in the Chrome Web Store directly, not our website.
  • Uninstall page, which Chrome opens when you remove the extension: https://link-extractor.com/uninstall/. The address includes the extension's version, your Chrome interface language and two fixed labels that show the extension opened the page (utm_source=extension, utm_medium=uninstall).

What these pages count

When the extension opens one of these pages, the page sends one event to PostHog, an analytics service, so we know how many people installed the extension, rated it low, or removed it, and why:

  • the welcome page: "extension installed", with your Chrome interface language, whether the extension icon is pinned and which instructions the page showed;
  • the "What went wrong?" page: "feedback opened", with the stars, where you gave them, the version and the language;
  • the uninstall page: "extension uninstalled", with the version and the language.

On the "What went wrong?" and uninstall pages you can also answer why. A click on a reason sends that reason (one of the six on the page), with the stars or the version; if you write a comment and press "Send", the page sends your text (at most 2,000 characters) with your reason and the same stars or version. Each answer also carries a random number for that one visit of the page, so that a changed answer replaces the earlier one. No answer is sent until you click; please do not write personal data in the comment.

Every event also carries the site's name (link-extractor.com), so that our own tests can be told apart, and the random ID described below. No event contains the extension's ID, the page address or a referrer. Unless you type them into a comment, no event contains your name, your email or your links. If you open these pages at their plain address rather than from the extension, they count no install, rating or removal; a reason or a comment you choose to send is still sent.

The random ID

So that the install, a rating and the removal of one installation can be seen as one path, the website keeps a random ID ("le_id") in its own storage in your browser (the website's local storage on link-extractor.com, not in the extension), created the first time one of these pages sends an event. The same ID goes with each event from that browser profile. It is not derived from anything about you or your computer, it is never put in an address and the extension never sees it. Removing the extension does not delete it, because it belongs to the website. Clearing the site data of link-extractor.com in Chrome's settings deletes it; then the next event gets a new one and is no longer linked to the earlier ones. Besides this ID, the pages store only a mark in the tab's session storage, so that reloading a page does not count it twice. They set no cookies.

Where the data goes

Events go to PostHog's US cloud (us.i.posthog.com), so they are processed in the United States. PostHog receives your IP address and may use it to estimate an approximate location (PostHog's privacy policy). Events are not tied to a person profile. The pages are hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, may keep standard server logs such as IP addresses and the addresses of the pages requested (GitHub's privacy statement).

The extension itself still sends nothing: it only tells Chrome which page to open, and the links you extract never leave your browser.

Payments

There are none. The extension is free and has no paid features.

Children

The extension collects no data from anyone, including children. The counters on our website do not ask for anyone's age, name or email. Link Extractor is not intended for children.
